Abstract

Acquiring diverse whole-body dexterous manipulation data for humanoids remains a fundamental challenge in character animation and robotics. Existing pipelines rely on expensive mocap data collected from many subjects and require retargeting to a unified humanoid shape, which limits scalable data construction and often yields physically inconsistent interactions (e.g., unstable contacts).

We present ManipSynth, a unified framework for whole-body loco-manipulation synthesis, with compositional generation of sparse whole-body motion, object-centric grasp anchors, and contact-aware interaction transitions. Then, we scale data generation through randomized pre-grasp motions, grasp poses, and object trajectories. ManipSynth achieves approximately 5× higher contact coverage and lower average penetration than OMOMO across representative objects.

To test whether better contact-consistent data improve scalable control learning, we train ManipTracker, a general-purpose Humanoid-Object Interaction (HOI) tracking policy, on purely synthetic demonstrations. Under the same object set, number of training trajectories, the same ManipTracker trained on ManipSynth demonstrates stronger generalization than when trained on mocap data, while achieving over 90% training success.

Part I, grasp anchor synthesis. Grasp poses are generated for each hand and object, optimized for force closure and filtered. Two-hand grasps of large objects are scored as pairs. Each clip shows a grasp from the front and from above.

Part II, body motion synthesis. A sparse tracker generates whole-body motion from any subset of three controls: the root, the left wrist and the right wrist.

Combine. Anchors convert object motion into wrist targets, and the tracker fills in the body: synthetic HOI trajectories for pick-place and handover, with one hand or two, on small and large objects. One of about 400 frames takes 5.4 s to generate. Each clip shows a demonstration from two views.

ManipTracker learns one HOI tracking policy in simulation from the synthetic data alone, with a single imitation reward for every skill. Left: the ManipSynth reference. Right: the physically simulated humanoid tracking it.

Beyond the paper: other robots. The same task synthesized for a Unitree G1 with Revo2 hands and for an H2 with Sharpa Wave hands: references that pass the pipeline's checks, not rollouts of a policy. The last clip shows a G1 interaction in simulation and on the real robot.
